New in v3:
Exit management — Partial exit at 1R (default books 50%, configurable), then the runner gets its stop moved to breakeven and trailed on either the EMA21 or VWAP (you pick). This is the single biggest upgrade to expectancy: you lock in a win early and let the rest run on a trail instead of a fixed target.
Regime filter — Uses ADX to classify the day. Above the threshold (default 20) → trend mode, takes the ORB/VWAP/EMA confluence entries. Below → range mode, which flips the logic and fades stretched moves back to VWAP using std-dev bands (plotted as dots so you can see them). This is what the original plan called "decide the regime first" — now automated.
Risk guardrails — Daily loss limit (stops trading once realised loss crosses ₹5,000 default), max trades/day cap (default 4), and a cooldown that blocks re-entry for N bars after a loss. These three together prevent the revenge-trading / death-by-a-thousand-cuts pattern that kills intraday F&O accounts.
Dashboard — Now shows live regime, ADX, VIX/qty, trades today, day P&L, total trades, win rate, and profit factor.
A few honest notes:

The trailing logic updates the runner's stop bar-by-bar via repeated strategy.exit calls — standard Pine pattern, but in the backtester a trail referenced to EMA/VWAP can exit later than you'd expect on fast reversals. Compare trailed vs. fixed-target results before committing.
The range-fade mode is the least-tested idea here conceptually — fading is lower win-rate by nature and depends heavily on the ADX threshold. Backtest trend-only first (set ADX threshold very low to force trend mode always), then enable regime and see if the fade trades actually add or subtract.
All the v2 caveats still hold: backtest the futures contract, the ₹20 commission understates real F&O cost, and don't curve-fit the parameters to maximize a small-sample backtest.
